RESEARCH LIBRARY

What accessibility barriers do people with CP face?

These findings describe environmental and information barriers, not clinical symptoms. The studies were conducted outside Kingston, and should not be interpreted as statistics about Kingston residents.

Evidence from published research

A 2025 Brazilian study of 109 children with cerebral palsy documented barriers involving access to information, physical spaces, community safety, sensory environments, programs and transportation.

A separate 2024 study of 145 children and adolescents with CP reported information and equipment availability challenges. Different samples cannot be combined into a Kingston estimate.

Read the 2025 study ↗

What we still need to learn in Kingston

We hope to gather voluntary, consent-based feedback about transportation, communication access, accessible washrooms, building entrances and community participation.

Current status: No Kingston-specific CP survey results have been collected or verified for this website.

THE STORY BEHIND THE PROJECT

About Kingston CP Connect

My name is Hassan Amir. In 2024, my brother experienced a stroke and was subsequently diagnosed with cerebral palsy. This experience changed the way my family and I understood the challenges people with cerebral palsy face, especially when it comes to accessibility, communication, and finding reliable information.

Inspired by my brother’s experience, we developed Kingston CP Connect to support individuals with cerebral palsy in Kingston, Ontario. Our goal is to make accurate information easier to access, help people navigate their community, and provide practical communication tools.

This is an evolving community project. We hope to improve it through input from people with CP and their families.

“A more accessible Kingston is a stronger, more inclusive community for everyone.”— Hassan Amir, Founder

Made for Kingston, Ontario. This prototype does not replace professional advice or a personal accessibility assessment.
